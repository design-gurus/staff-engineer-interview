# The Staff Engineer Interview Loop

A staff engineer loop usually has three stages: a recruiter call, one or two screens, and an onsite of four or five rounds. A hiring committee then decides two things separately: whether to hire, and at which level. Most candidates prepare for the first decision and lose the second.

The details below are commonly reported by candidates. No company publishes a fixed loop, and teams inside one company differ.

## The stages

| Stage | Length | What happens | What it decides |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recruiter call | 30 minutes | Background, target level, timeline | Which level the loop is calibrated for |
| Technical screen | 45 to 60 minutes | A coding problem or a short design discussion | Whether to invest in an onsite |
| Hiring manager screen | 45 minutes | Your largest projects and why you are looking | Whether your scope matches the role |
| Onsite | 4 to 5 rounds | System design, project deep dive, behavioral, coding | The evidence the committee reads |
| Hiring committee | No candidate present | Interviewers' written feedback is reviewed | Hire or no hire, and the level |

## The onsite rounds

| Round | Typical count | Staff-level evidence it produces |
|---|---|---|
| [System design](system-design.md) | 1 or 2 | You framed a vague problem, named non-goals, and planned a migration |
| [Project deep dive](project-deep-dive.md) | 1 | You made the hard decisions in a project that crossed team boundaries |
| [Behavioral](behavioral.md) | 1 or 2 | You influenced people who did not report to you |
| [Coding](coding.md) | 1 | You write correct, readable code at senior level |

Some companies add a written exercise, such as a short technical strategy document completed at home. Others replace the second design round with an architecture review of an existing system.

## How the level is decided

Each interviewer writes feedback with a recommended level. The committee looks for staff evidence in at least two rounds, and the project deep dive and behavioral rounds carry the most weight for that decision. A strong design round with weak scope evidence is the usual route to a senior offer.

Three questions summarize what the committee needs answered:

1. Has this person led work that involved several teams?
2. Did this person make decisions, or carry out decisions that others made?
3. Do the decisions still look sound after one or two years?

Every answer you give in the loop should supply evidence for at least one of these.

## What to settle in the recruiter call

The recruiter call sets the target level, so treat it as part of the interview.

- State the level you are interviewing for and ask whether the loop is calibrated for it.
- Ask which rounds are in the loop and whether a project deep dive is one of them.
- Describe your scope as evidence: the number of teams affected, the duration, and one measured result.

A sentence such as "I led the consolidation of three job schedulers used by 26 teams over 14 months" gives the recruiter something to record. "I work on platform infrastructure" does not.

## A reasonable preparation split

| Area | Share of time | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Project deep dive | 30 percent | It is unique to you and cannot be studied from a book |
| Behavioral stories | 25 percent | Staff scope must be visible in every story |
| System design | 30 percent | The staff additions take practice to deliver in 45 minutes |
| Coding | 15 percent | The bar is senior level, so maintenance is enough for most candidates |
