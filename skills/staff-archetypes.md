# Staff Engineer Archetypes

Staff engineer is one title that covers several different jobs. Will Larson described four common forms of the role, which he calls archetypes: Tech Lead, Architect, Solver, and Right Hand. His original guide is at [staffeng.com](https://staffeng.com/guides/staff-archetypes/). Knowing which archetype a role expects tells you which stories to prepare.

## The four archetypes

| Archetype | What the person owns | How success is measured | Typical setting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tech Lead | The technical direction and delivery of one team or a few related teams | The team ships the right work on time | Most common; partners with one manager |
| Architect | The direction and quality of one technical area, such as the API or the data platform | The area stays coherent as many teams change it | Larger companies with shared platforms |
| Solver | One hard problem at a time, chosen by leadership | The problem is resolved, then the person moves to the next | Companies that plan around individuals |
| Right Hand | Whatever a senior leader most needs attention on | The leader's organization runs better | Organizations of several hundred engineers |

## One company, four staff engineers

Consider a marketplace with 400 engineers in 45 teams.

- **Tech Lead.** Leads the checkout team's move to a new payment provider, breaking the work into pieces that six engineers deliver over two quarters.
- **Architect.** Owns the event schema used by 180 services, reviews every change to it, and writes the policy for versioning.
- **Solver.** Spends four months finding why search latency doubles every Monday, then moves to a data loss investigation.
- **Right Hand.** Works with the vice president of engineering on the three problems that cross the most teams, such as incident response.

Each of these is a staff engineer. Their weeks look almost nothing alike.

## Matching the archetype to the interview

| Archetype | Stories to emphasize | Round that matters most |
|---|---|---|
| Tech Lead | Scoping, sequencing, and growing other engineers | Behavioral and project deep dive |
| Architect | Long-lived decisions, standards, and migrations | System design |
| Solver | Depth, diagnosis, and results under uncertainty | Project deep dive |
| Right Hand | Organization-wide problems and working with executives | Behavioral |

## How to learn which archetype a role expects

Ask the recruiter or the hiring manager three questions before the onsite.

1. Does this role belong to one team, or does it work across several?
2. Who will this person work with most closely each week?
3. What is the first problem you would want this person to take on?

"One team, the engineering manager, and the delivery of our next platform" describes a Tech Lead. "Across the organization, the director, and whatever is most urgent" describes a Right Hand.

## Moving between archetypes

The archetypes are not permanent categories. Many engineers reach staff as a Tech Lead and later work as an Architect or a Solver. In an interview, name the archetype you have mostly worked in, and show one story from a second archetype to demonstrate range.

Avoid claiming all four. A candidate who says "I do everything" gives the interviewer no clear picture to record.

## Common mistakes

- Preparing only Tech Lead stories for an Architect role.
- Describing yourself with a title and not with the work you owned.
- Treating the Right Hand as an assistant. The role carries the leader's scope and requires independent judgment.
