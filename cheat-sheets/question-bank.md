# Staff Engineer Interview Questions

This page lists 50 questions that staff engineer candidates commonly report, in seven groups. Each group begins with one line on what a staff-level answer adds. Prepare two stories per behavioral group, and practice each answer aloud in under three minutes.

## Scope

**A staff answer adds:** a problem that crossed team boundaries and that nobody assigned to you.

1. What is the largest technical problem you have owned?
2. Tell me about a problem you found that was outside your team's responsibility.
3. How do you decide which problems to work on?
4. Describe a project where you set the technical direction for several teams.
5. Tell me about work you chose not to do, and why.
6. What is the longest-lived technical decision you have made?
7. How has your scope changed in the last two years?

## Ambiguity

**A staff answer adds:** the measurable problem statement you wrote, and who agreed to it.

8. Tell me about a time you were given a goal with no clear requirements.
9. Describe a project where the problem turned out to be different from the request.
10. How do you start when nobody can tell you what success means?
11. Tell me about a time you had to decide with incomplete data.
12. Describe a time priorities changed in the middle of a project.
13. How did you estimate a project that had many unknowns?
14. Tell me about a time you stopped a project.

## Conflict

**A staff answer adds:** the criteria both sides agreed on, and what happened to the people who disagreed.

15. Tell me about a disagreement with another senior engineer.
16. Describe a time you disagreed with your manager's technical decision.
17. Tell me about a time you said no to a product lead.
18. Describe a design review that went badly.
19. Tell me about a time two teams wanted incompatible things.
20. When did you disagree and commit? What happened afterward?
21. Tell me about a time you changed your mind in a technical argument.

## Failure

**A staff answer adds:** a real cost, the information you had at the time, and the specific change you made afterward.

22. Tell me about a technical decision you got wrong.
23. Describe a project that missed its deadline.
24. Tell me about an incident you caused or could have prevented.
25. What is a migration or rollout that did not finish as planned?
26. Tell me about a time your estimate was badly wrong.
27. Describe feedback that was difficult to hear.
28. What would you do differently in your largest project?

## Influence

**A staff answer adds:** who had to agree, what each party objected to, and what you changed in response.

29. Tell me about a time you convinced a team that did not report to you.
30. How did you get a standard or a tool adopted across teams?
31. Describe a decision that was stuck. How did you move it?
32. Tell me about an engineer whose scope grew because of you.
33. How do you handle a team that will not migrate?
34. Tell me about a time you influenced a decision above your level.
35. Describe a document you wrote that changed what other teams built.

## System design

**A staff answer adds:** non-goals, cost and operational load, a migration plan, and how the design changes over three years.

36. "Scheduled jobs keep failing. Fix it."
37. Design a platform that deletes a user's data from 60 services within 30 days.
38. Our deploys take two hours. Design a faster pipeline for 45 teams.
39. Consolidate three messaging systems into one.
40. Design a rate limiting service that every internal API will use.
41. Move a 4 terabyte primary database to a new storage engine without downtime.
42. Design multi-region failover for an existing single-region product.
43. Design an internal feature flag platform, and decide whether to build or buy it.

## Project deep dive

**A staff answer adds:** the alternative to each decision, the numbers behind it, and its effect on other teams.

44. Describe the most complex project you have led, in eight minutes.
45. What alternatives did you consider, and why did you reject them?
46. Which decision in that project was yours alone?
47. What did the project cost to run after launch?
48. Who disagreed with the approach, and what did you do?
49. What broke after launch?
50. If you started again today, what would you change?

## How to use this page

1. Mark each behavioral question with the story you would use.
2. Find the groups with fewer than two stories and fill them first.
3. Record yourself on five questions, and check each answer for scope, a decision with alternatives, and the effect on other teams.
