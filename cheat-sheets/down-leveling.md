# Down-Leveling

Down-leveling is an offer one level below the level you interviewed for. The usual case is a senior offer after a staff loop. It rarely means the candidate performed badly. It means the loop found strong senior work and not enough evidence of staff scope.

## Why it happens

| Cause | What the feedback says | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Stories stay inside one team | "Strong execution, limited cross-team scope" | Behavioral |
| Decisions are not visible | "Unclear what the candidate personally decided" | Project deep dive |
| Design stops at the architecture | "Solid design, did not address migration or cost" | System design |
| The loop was calibrated for a lower level | "Meets the bar for senior" | Recruiter call |
| Title inflation | "Scope at previous company matches our senior level" | Resume and hiring manager screen |

Title inflation means a title at one company that corresponds to a lower level at another.

## Warning signs before the offer

- The recruiter says "we will determine the level after the loop."
- The loop has two coding rounds and no project deep dive.
- Interviewers ask about your code and never about other teams.
- The hiring manager describes a role with one team and one service.

When you see one of these, ask the recruiter directly which level the loop is calibrated for. The question is normal and costs nothing.

## Reducing the risk

1. State your target level in the first call and repeat it before the onsite.
2. Open every behavioral story with scope: teams, duration, and a measured result.
3. Say "I decided" when you decided. Use "we" only for the work the group did.
4. In the design round, reach non-goals, cost, and migration without being asked.
5. Choose a deep dive project that involved at least three teams.

## Your options when the offer is one level lower

| Option | When it fits | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Ask for a level review | One or two rounds lacked staff evidence that you can supply | Send a short written summary of scope the loop did not cover; ask whether an additional round is possible |
| Accept at the lower level | The company, team, and work are right, and promotion is realistic | Ask what staff evidence was missing and how promotion is assessed |
| Decline | The level matters more than this company | Continue other loops; keep the relationship with the recruiter |
| Use a competing offer | Another company has offered staff | Tell the recruiter the level of the other offer |

A level review does not always exist, and the answer is often no. Asking once, politely and with evidence, is still reasonable.

## A level review message

Keep it to five sentences.

> "Thank you for the offer. I interviewed for staff, and I would like to ask whether the committee can review the level. The loop did not cover the scheduler consolidation I led across 26 teams over 14 months, which reduced missed-run incidents from six a quarter to zero. I can provide a written summary or take an additional interview on it. If a review is not possible, I would value knowing which evidence was missing."

## Accepting a lower level

Accepting is not a failure. Promotion from senior to staff inside a large company is commonly reported to take two years or more, so weigh that time honestly. Three questions help:

- Does the team have staff-scope problems available?
- Has anyone on the team been promoted to staff recently?
- Will your manager state in writing what staff evidence looks like?
