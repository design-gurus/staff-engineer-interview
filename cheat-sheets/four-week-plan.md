# Four-Week Staff Engineer Interview Plan

This plan assumes 8 to 10 hours per week and an engineer who already works at senior level. It spends most of the time on the project deep dive and the behavioral stories, because those rounds decide the level. Each week ends with a finish condition, which is a concrete test that the week's work is complete.

## The plan at a glance

| Week | Focus | Hours | Finish condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Evidence: scope, stories, and the project | 8 | A story bank of six stories and one chosen deep dive project |
| 2 | Project deep dive | 10 | An eight-minute walkthrough recorded, with a depth map for every decision |
| 3 | System design at staff level | 10 | Three designs completed in 45 minutes each, all reaching migration and cost |
| 4 | Mock loops and gaps | 8 to 10 | One full mock loop and a one-page summary of your scope |

## Week 1: gather the evidence

1. Read [senior vs staff engineer](../skills/senior-vs-staff.md) and answer the five self-check questions.
2. List six to eight projects and incidents from the last five years.
3. For each, write the scope, your decision, the alternatives, and a measured result.
4. Score your projects with the sheet in [project deep dive](../rounds/project-deep-dive.md) and choose one.
5. Take the coding test in [coding](../rounds/coding.md) to set your coding hours.

Find missing numbers now. Old dashboards, incident reports, and design documents usually contain them.

## Week 2: the project deep dive

1. Write the project as context, constraints, choices, results on one page.
2. Build the depth map: for each decision, the alternative, the numbers, and the second-order effect.
3. Write your mistake in four parts: the decision, the information at the time, the cost, and the change.
4. Record an eight-minute walkthrough and listen for every "we" that should be "I".
5. Ask a colleague to question you for 30 minutes and note each question you could not answer.

## Week 3: system design

1. Read [system design](../rounds/system-design.md) and learn the 45-minute time budget.
2. Complete three designs from the [question bank](question-bank.md), each under a timer.
3. After each one, check six items: problem statement, non-goals, evolution plan, cost and load, migration, and pre-mortem.
4. Read [technical judgment](../skills/technical-judgment.md) and practice one debt calculation and one build or buy comparison.

A design that ends at the architecture diagram does not meet the finish condition. Repeat it until the last ten minutes cover migration and risk.

## Week 4: mock loops

1. Run one full mock loop: design, deep dive, and behavioral, 45 minutes each.
2. Practice ten questions from the [question bank](question-bank.md) aloud, two per behavioral group.
3. Write a one-page scope summary for the recruiter: teams, duration, results.
4. Review [common mistakes](common-mistakes.md) and [down-leveling](down-leveling.md).
5. Confirm the target level and the list of rounds with your recruiter.

## Weekly coding maintenance

| Week 1 test result | Coding hours per week |
|---|---|
| Two or three of three problems solved | 1 to 2, about three problems |
| One or none solved | 3 to 4, taken from the design hours in weeks 1 and 4 |

## If you have less time

| Time available | What to keep |
|---|---|
| Two weeks | Weeks 1 and 2 in the first week, weeks 3 and 4 in the second, at 15 hours each |
| One week | The story bank, the deep dive walkthrough, and one timed design |
| Two days | The deep dive walkthrough and the three layers in [behavioral](../rounds/behavioral.md) |
